# Retirement distributions and Social Security benefits

The 2023–2025 path accepts encrypted 1099-R, SSA-1099, RRB-1099 and tax-exempt interest sources, calculates supported taxable retirement income and ordinary taxable Social Security benefits, and connects those amounts to Form 1040, withholding, AGI, QBI's taxable-income limit and NIIT MAGI. It includes source workpapers and the annual printed form annotations. This is bounded draft preparation, not complete retirement planning, agency certification or filing approval.

## Supported calculation

`individual.retirementIncome` contains `distributions`, `benefits`, `taxExemptInterestPayers`, the separate-return all-year living arrangement, and explicit scope/source reviews. The browser editor uses the exported record factories. IDs are unique across its sources; spouse records require a joint return. Original source cents, codes and explanations remain available in the encrypted record and PDF workpaper.

Retirement distribution gross amounts determine taxable income under these methods:

* Fully taxable traditional IRA or qualified employer-plan distributions with normal/death code 7/4, no unrecovered basis and reviewed additional-tax obligations. A source box 2a amount, if entered, must reconcile to the gross distribution.
* Qualified Roth IRA code Q distributions, with source/qualification review and no taxable amount. Code Q represents the payer's five-year and age/death/disability qualification. Other Roth codes and designated Roth accounts need additional treatment.
* Eligible tax-free traditional-IRA/qualified-plan rollovers. Direct code G rollovers must cover the entire source distribution and have zero withholding. Supported indirect rollovers require normal code 7, an in-year receipt, a completion date within 60 days, a compatible destination, and an eligible amount no greater than gross. Taxable income is gross less the completed rollover. A source box 2a for an indirect rollover reconciles to the original gross because the payer does not account for the recipient's subsequent rollover. Source references and rollover dates/amounts produce an explanatory statement, including next-year completion or a transfer to an employer plan.

Rollovers require review of ownership, eligible assets, the once-per-year rule, prohibited RMDs and other limitations; the program does not infer eligibility from the amount alone. Inherited-account exceptions, early-distribution rollovers, partial direct rollovers, waivers, loans and Roth conversions are outside this limited method. An unsupported selection remains saved and blocks affected outputs.

SSA/RRB net benefits are calculated from source box 3 less box 4, reconciled to optional reported box 5. The program includes only Social Security-equivalent RRB-1099 benefits; RRB-1099-R pensions require separate treatment. It never subtracts Medicare premiums from taxable-benefit input. Positive net benefits are processed through the annual Form 1040 Social Security Benefits Worksheet, using wages, taxable interest, ordinary dividends, taxable IRA/pension income, capital income, Schedule 1 income, tax-exempt interest and supported adjustments such as half the SE tax. The worksheet applies the joint $32,000/$44,000 thresholds, the $25,000/$34,000 thresholds for other qualifying filers, or the special married-separate rule for spouses who lived together. Qualifying surviving spouses use the $25,000/$34,000 benefit thresholds, despite using joint ordinary-tax rates.

Tax-exempt payer records supply Form 1040 line 2a and the Social Security worksheet. They do not increase ordinary taxable income or NIIT investment income. Taxable retirement/benefit amounts do increase AGI and therefore can affect the QBI taxable-income ceiling and NIIT MAGI. They do not become self-employment earnings, QBI-qualified business income or NIIT investment income. Known federal withholding from 1099-R box 4, SSA-1099 box 6 and RRB-1099 box 10 enters Form 1040 line 25b, separately from W-2 withholding and Additional Medicare withholding.

## Unresolved treatments and source boundaries

No entered final taxable-amount override is accepted. The optional reported box 2a is a source reconciliation field. Recorded after-tax cost triggers additional basis work; it is not simply subtracted. Traditional IRA basis/Form 8606, the annuity Simplified Method/General Rule, nonqualified plans, early-distribution tax/Form 5329, QCD/HFD, Roth conversions, disability amounts treated as wages, disaster relief, corrective distributions, required-distribution shortfalls, SEP/SIMPLE treatment and special lump-sum elections require further implementation/review.

The supported Social Security worksheet excludes IRA-deduction interactions, foreign/adoption/savings-bond/Puerto Rico exclusions, special disability/treaty/nonresident exclusions, prior-year lump-sum payments and unresolved write-in adjustments. Repayments exceeding benefits require Publication 915 deduction/credit work. Source failures and incompatible methods keep taxable benefits, dependent AGI/tax/QBI and balance outputs unavailable. Source/withholding review separately controls payments; a known withholding source is not discarded solely because its income treatment is unresolved. General age-65/blindness, dependent and other standard-deduction adjustments remain separate existing preparation gates; no age is inferred from receiving a pension or Social Security.

Tax year 2026 has an explicit annual gate for selected retirement records. Source records remain available, but 2025 worksheets/annotations are not substituted. Returns without the optional retirement record preserve their existing behavior.

## Official form behavior and evidence

The gross/taxable reporting rules distinguish a single fully taxable distribution (taxable line only) from IRA multiple distributions, Roth distributions and rollovers (gross and taxable lines). Pensions with a rollover have gross and taxable amounts. The 2023/2024 forms require written “Rollover” next to the IRA/pension taxable line and “D” beside Social Security benefits for married-separate filers who lived apart all year. Those blanks have no AcroFields for these annotations, so the renderer places the required text at the verified printed coordinates. The 2025 form instead uses the rollover checkboxes on lines 4c/5c and the lived-apart checkbox on line 6d. The lump-sum election checkbox on line 6c is never inferred or selected.

`test/retirement-reference.json` pins the downloaded bytes/SHA-256 of all fifteen annual sources. Its supported published case is Publication 915 Example 1: $18,600 pension, $9,400 wages, $990 taxable interest, $5,980 benefits and $2,990 taxable benefits. Other threshold/living-arrangement vectors are explicitly synthetic. Publication 915 examples involving an IRA deduction or savings-bond exclusion are not claimed as supported full-return scenarios.

`test/retirement-income.test.mjs` verifies source-based calculations, annual published examples, rollovers, basis/code conflicts, thresholds, source bounds, unknown propagation, QBI/NIIT integration and withholding. `test/retirement-pdf.test.mjs` checks actual flattened annual PDF text at the original row coordinates and verifies required annotations, checkbox paths and blank unresolved fields. `packages/secure-tax-client/test/retirement-editor.test.mjs` exercises the real editor module with synthetic DOM events, source fields, save callback data, reopening and private-value clearing. This is programmatic engineering evidence, not desktop visual acceptance or government acceptance.
