Overview

What it is, and why it's different

Blue-collar and shift businesses lose real money in the seams between four apps: one to schedule, one to clock, one to run payroll, and a spreadsheet to figure out what each job actually cost in labor. Shuug closes those seams. A clock-in is the same hour that fills the timesheet, prices the job, and feeds the pay run — captured once, used everywhere. That means labor cost is real (not a guess), overtime is caught before it happens, and payroll comes from the hours people actually worked instead of a hand-typed rekey.

In depth

How it really works, no hand-waving

Capture the hour once, use it four ways

The single biggest source of payroll error is re-entry — hours copied from a clock to a timesheet to a payroll app, losing accuracy at every hop. Here the hour is captured once at clock-in and referenced everywhere it's needed.

That one fact fixes a chain of problems: timesheets are automatically right, job cost is automatically real, and the pay run is automatically from true hours. There's nothing to reconcile between systems because there's one system.

Catch overtime and coverage before they cost you

Overtime and understaffing are both expensive, and both are avoidable if you can see them coming. The schedule and the live clock together give you that foresight:

  • Overtime risk flags an employee trending past their hours before the shift, while you can still adjust.
  • Coverage gaps show on the rota so a shift isn't discovered empty an hour before open.
  • Hours-by-job tells you which jobs are eating labor, so you bid the next one right.

Questions

Do I still need a separate payroll app?
No — timesheets flow into a pay run inside Shuug, posted to the books. It replaces the schedule/clock/payroll app stack.
Does labor cost show up on jobs automatically?
